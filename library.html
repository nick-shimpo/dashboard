1Carcinid Sensory Library
▶
Carcinids as terraforming tools — servitude-driven, symbiotic with local beetles, adaptive worker caste. Wrong-but-purposeful. Never mindless.
Sounds · d10
  1. A clicking that pauses when you pause, then resumes half a beat later.
  2. Wet chitin sliding across metal — the sound a wet boot makes dragged over a grill.
  3. Something breathing in a pattern that isn't breathing. Four short, one long. Repeat.
  4. A shriek cut short — not stopped, swallowed. Somewhere below you.
  5. Legs moving in unison. Not marching. Coordinated.
  6. The beetle-rattle of local wildlife, but louder. Closer. Bigger.
  7. A chorus of clicks that suddenly goes silent all at once — as if something gave an order.
  8. Scraping on the far side of a wall, moving in a straight line, too fast for its size.
  9. The Shriek — half insect cry, half something trying to imitate a human voice.
  10. Silence. Complete. The station's background hum is still there, but the carcinids — which you've been hearing for hours — have stopped. They are listening.
Movement · d10
  1. It moves sideways faster than it moves forwards.
  2. It pauses mid-stride, considers, then changes direction.
  3. Four limbs working together, two working against each other — the body hasn't decided what it is yet.
  4. It flows over obstacles the way water flows — no hesitation, no plan, just gone.
  5. Two of them pass each other in a corridor, touch mandibles for half a second, separate.
  6. One climbs a wall. Another watches from below. Neither is hunting you yet.
  7. It drags something. A boot. A helmet. It doesn't seem to know what the object is for.
  8. It stops. Turns its head — no, its whole front section — toward a wall where there is nothing. Waits. Moves on.
  9. It doesn't run from the light. It just closes the parts of itself that are vulnerable to it.
  10. It's not moving at all. Has it ever been? Or did you only think it moved because something else did, nearby?
Residue / trace · d10
  1. A smear of something oily on the wall, twenty centimetres wide, waist height, running for metres.
  2. Shed chitin — but not one piece. Many. As if something moulted, then moulted again, then again, getting larger each time.
  3. Beetle carapaces in a pile. The local wildlife. Stripped clean. Arranged in a rough circle.
  4. Claw scores on a bulkhead, not random — parallel, deliberate, like a count. Or a message.
  5. A sticky film coating the floor for three metres around a corpse. It smells like crushed leaves and ammonia.
  6. A human uniform, empty, lying in the open. No blood. No body. The boots are laced.
  7. Pools of clear fluid that aren't water. They have a faint iridescence. They are slightly warm.
  8. A nest — not of carcinids. Of beetles. But the beetles are bigger than they should be. Shell pattern is almost geometric.
  9. A patch of the wall where the metal has softened. Not melted. Softened. You can press a thumbprint into it.
  10. Something that looks like a cocoon. It's breathing very slowly. Whatever's inside is the wrong shape for a carcinid.
Uncanny details · sparingly
Big moments. Deploy deliberately.
  1. You see one up close for the first time. Its eye moves independently of the rest of its body. It's looking past you.
  2. It has a scrap of marine webbing fused to its carapace. You recognise the stitching. It's ours.
  3. When it dies, the body doesn't stop moving for almost ten seconds. It's not twitching. It's finishing.
  4. A carcinid freezes in place. You hear the Shriek far away. The carcinid turns and walks, in a dead straight line, back toward the reactor.
  5. One of them limps. A broken leg. The other carcinids walk around it without helping. It keeps going anyway.
  6. You find a carcinid that looks almost finished — but there's still skin underneath the chitin. Human skin. It twitches when you touch it.
The Mimic · single-use birth sequence
  • The Shriek from the reactor is louder than you've ever heard it. Then silence.
  • The silence lasts a full minute. You count.
  • When the carcinids return — and they do, in numbers — they pass you without stopping. They're not hunting tonight.
  • In the distance, something walks upright. Its silhouette is wearing a marine helmet. It doesn't move like a marine.
2Heron Station Set Dressing
▶
Dam-and-hydroelectric station on a jungle world in a cataclysmic storm, mid-invasion, terraforming bleeding into infrastructure.
Stairwells · Team Alpha problem
  • The emergency lighting here runs on backup power. It flickers when the reactor does. It hasn't stopped flickering all night.
  • Clicks echo. You can't tell which floor they're on. The stairwell is a tuning fork for horror.
  • A blood smear on a handrail, waist height, running the length of two flights. Someone held on hard, for a long time.
  • The stairwell door two levels down is welded shut from the inside. Someone's writing is scrawled next to it — a single word, too dark to read without a light.
  • A ration wrapper caught in the metalwork between flights. Still faintly warm.
  • Air moves up the stairwell. That means a door is open somewhere above. Or below.
  • A rivet has popped out of the wall and is lying on the step. The walls here were never supposed to flex.
  • You reach a landing. The number painted on the wall is wrong — it repeats the level below. Someone painted over the original.
Reactor corridors · post-sluice
  • The pressure has dropped but the walls are still warm. The metal ticks as it cools — a sound like many small things walking.
  • A service robot sits in the middle of the corridor, powered on, its head turning very slowly from side to side. It won't respond to commands.
  • Signage peeling from the bulkheads. Underneath the peel, the metal is softer. Pliable. Warm.
  • A puddle of coolant on the floor. In the puddle: a reflection that shows the corridor behind you, but with something standing in it.
  • You hear the reactor's pulse now — a low, biological thump-thump-thump. It was not biological three days ago.
  • The wall murals that Maas's team painted for morale — colonists waving, children, a sunset — are discoloured. The sunset is the wrong colour. Orange where it used to be yellow. No one painted over it.
The hangar · Willis problem
  • Tyre tracks in the mud outside — one set of ATV tracks leading away from the hangar. Fresh.
  • The soldier Brookman shot is still in the doorway. Someone has half-dragged them inside. Nobody is doing the rest.
  • Radio chatter from the outer perimeter is intermittent. Words you catch: fuel, breaking, not them.
  • The hangar bay doors won't close all the way — warped in the storm — leaving a ten-centimetre gap at the bottom. Rain is coming in.
  • Someone has stacked crates against the inner door. The stack is neat. Too neat for an emergency.
  • A family photo on a workbench, face-down. Next to a half-eaten meal.
  • The dropship crew is on the radio every twenty minutes now. Each call is shorter than the last.
Outdoors · storm + jungle
  • The rain doesn't fall straight. It comes in horizontal sheets. You can hear it hitting the far side of the station like gunfire.
  • Jungle wildlife is silent. Has been for hours. You don't realise how loud it used to be until it isn't.
  • Lightning strikes — not above, but horizontal — a line of light running between two points on the ridge, lasting two full seconds.
  • The mud is warm. Warmer than the rain. The ground itself is putting out heat.
  • Vines have grown across a section of path that was clear yesterday. You can see boot prints underneath them.
  • You pass a piece of station equipment — a generator, a pump — that has been enveloped in biological matter. It is still running.
  • A beetle the size of your palm sits on your shoulder. It does not fly away when you brush at it. You have to scrape it off. It leaves a mark.
Reactor chamber · if anyone returns
  • The Mother's punctures are larger now. Something is going in as well as coming out.
  • The carcinids cocooned in the walls have changed position since you were last here. Not much. Just enough.
  • Franco's cocoon is smaller. It is not smaller because he has escaped.
  • The radiation counter clicks slower here, not faster. She is drinking it.
  • There is new growth on the floor. Not plants. Not carcinids. Something else. A third category.
3Terraforming Evidence
▶
Details that don't fit one category. Individually weird. Collectively, the horror telescope. Let players accumulate them.
Biological · process leaking
  • A fern growing out of a wall socket. Leaves the wrong colour — grey-blue.
  • Mould on the ceiling that forms perfect hexagonal patterns.
  • A cluster of beetles moving in deliberate formation across a floor. They change direction together.
  • A dead rat (station-kept, shipped from Earth). Its fur has changed colour in patches. It was not like this yesterday.
  • Human hair growing from a patch of moss. Just hair. Not attached to anything.
  • The drinking water tastes slightly sweet now. Only the water from certain taps.
  • A potted plant in Edem's lab has grown through its pot and into the floor plating.
Structural · station responding
  • A bulkhead door that swings open and shut rhythmically, on no one's command, every nine seconds.
  • Paint peeling in the shape of a spiral, all over the station, all in the same direction.
  • A section of corridor where the gravity feels slightly wrong. Not wrong enough to fall. Wrong enough to notice.
  • The station's automated announcements are playing at half speed. No one has changed the settings.
  • A maintenance hatch that has grown shut — metal fused with something organic, sealing the seam.
  • The emergency lighting in one corridor is now green. It was white when you arrived.
Behavioural · things that were alive
  • A flock of local birds flew into the side of the dam in formation at dawn. The bodies are still there.
  • The station's dog (yes, there was a dog) has not barked since the storm started. It hasn't moved from its bed. It is alive.
  • Carcinid corpses lie in deliberate arrangements. Not piles. Shapes. A circle. A line pointing west. A spiral.
  • A marine's body has been positioned. Not dragged. Positioned. Arms crossed. Eyes closed. Respect? Or study?
  • A beetle that should live for three weeks is still alive at twelve weeks. Bigger. Quieter. Watches you.
Technological · humans noticing
  • Edem's lab has research notes tacked to the wall. The last entry reads: "It's not infection. It's integration."
  • A security feed replays a fifteen-second loop of an empty corridor. Every fifteen seconds, for a single frame, there's something in the corridor. Then gone.
  • A radiation detector reading higher than the reactor, in a room nowhere near the reactor.
  • The long-range comms array keeps picking up a carrier signal from outside the station. The signal has no content. It is just there. It is getting stronger.
  • Someone — Hinton? — deleted the seismic logs from the last three weeks. Only those. The backup is also gone.
Horror telescope · reveal fragments (use late)
  • The pattern of the mother's punctures, seen from above, matches the mountain ridge line visible from the hangar window.
  • The carcinids, in their circle-arrangements, face the mountain. All of them. Every time.
  • A surveyor's map in Maas's office is marked with small red dots. The dots cluster around the mountain. Far more than around the station.
  • The local beetle life, held up against the preserved sample Edem had from before landing, is subtly different. Bigger mandibles. An extra segment. It took weeks to happen.
  • The storm, on satellite weather, isn't a normal storm front. The shape of it is a spiral. The spiral's centre is directly over the mountain.

1The Shriek · Control Signal
▶
Elders' control frequency. Passes through carcinids, transformed matter, station infrastructure. The connective element of the terraforming horror.
How it manifests
  • A sound — somewhere between a cicada's cry and a tearing metal sheet, at a frequency that makes fillings ache.
  • A vibration — felt in the floor, the walls, the chest, before it becomes audible.
  • A pause — every living thing in earshot stops for half a second, then continues. Including the players, if they listen carefully to themselves.
  • A pressure behind the eyes, lasting two or three seconds, that makes you want to lie down.
  • An absence — for a moment after the Shriek, the station's normal sounds don't resume. Then they do. But not all of them.
Observable consequences
  • Carcinids in the area change behaviour simultaneously. Stop. Reorient. Move as a unit.
  • Local beetle life — wherever there are beetles — rises into the air in a column.
  • The Mother pulses. You can see it from the reactor chamber entry: a wave of motion through her bulk.
  • Lights dim. Not flicker. Dim, as if something was drawing power through them.
  • Someone's personal electronics glitch — helmet HUD, radio, medscanner. Brief. Unexplained.
  • Plants and fungal growth in the corridors lean toward the mountain for several seconds afterwards.
Escalation · session pacing tool
  • Distant · felt more than heard, once per scene. Baseline.
  • Closer · audible, 1-second duration, every scene. Something responding.
  • Near · overwhelming, 3-second duration. Everything reacts visibly. Peril escalation moments.
  • The Chorus · multiple Shrieks overlapping, from multiple directions. End-of-scene event.
Drop-verbatim lines
  • "The station hums at its usual frequency. Then — for a breath — it doesn't."
  • "You feel it before you hear it. A pressure. Then the sound."
  • "Every carcinid you can see stops. They are listening. None of them are listening to you."
  • "The Shriek is farther this time. But lasts longer."
  • "It's coming from inside the mountain."
2Transformation Stages
▶
Mother transforms local biology via radiation + Shriek-delivered instruction. When players see a transformed thing, pick a stage.
01Contact
Subtle. Unnoticed unless you're looking.
  • A patch of plant growth where there shouldn't be any.
  • An animal (beetle, rat, bird) that has changed colour.
  • A hair, a feather, a scale that is slightly the wrong shape.
  • Station metal that has softened — only microns of give, but noticeable to the touch.
  • Drinking water that tastes slightly sweet.
02Integration
Still recognisable but clearly wrong. Where most discoveries land.
  • Fungal growth in geometric patterns.
  • A beetle twice its normal size, moving on its own without its swarm.
  • A rat with its fur changed to chitinous plating over the spine.
  • A wall section that has grown together with a carcinid corpse, sharing surface material.
  • A human body that died three days ago, but the hair and nails have kept growing — and not just growing: branching.
03Symbiosis
Two things have become one. Rarer. More horrifying.
  • A carcinid fused to a service panel, its legs still moving, the panel reading out status codes.
  • A marine body partly cocooned but still breathing in rhythm with the mother's pulse.
  • Vines from the jungle that have grown into the dam's concrete and now flower in time with the Shriek.
  • A dead dog that is upright, walking in a slow loop, clearly not alive but responding to something.
04Directed
Under direct Shriek control. The elders' tools.
  • The Mimic (already in canon).
  • A carcinid that doesn't attack — it herds, waits, opens doors for the players and closes them afterwards.
  • A human voice on the radio saying things a carcinid wouldn't know — if a carcinid were using the radio.
  • Something walking upright in the rain outside the hangar. You can't tell what it used to be.
05Integrated
Full conversion. No memory of being separate. Final-scenario territory.
  • Reserve for the alien ship. Anything at Stage 5 is a node of the elders' will. The Mother is approaching Stage 5. Hinton wants to achieve it voluntarily.
3The Elders · Lore
▶
They never appear directly. Inferred through evidence, never encounter.
Physical traces
  • A mural in the alien ship's outer chamber: figures tall, unchanging, too many joints. Carved, not painted. Older than Earth civilization.
  • A skull-like fragment on a shelf in Hinton's private quarters — not human, not carcinid, older than both. He studied it.
  • Geometry in the alien ship that doesn't use right angles. Every structure is curved, nested, spiralling.
  • Writing — if it is writing — that repeats the same eight symbols in different configurations, everywhere.
Behavioural inference
  • The terraforming is precise. It doesn't destroy. It modifies. This is a choice.
  • The process leverages existing biology. It is thrifty. It takes what works and bends it.
  • Carcinids are not the elders. They are tools. The tools are adaptable because the elders are not.
  • The elders have been doing this for a very long time. There are other worlds. There have always been other worlds.
Hinton lines · late scenario only
  • "They don't think like us. They don't have to."
  • "They've done this a thousand times. Samsa VI is a small piece of a very long pattern."
  • "They don't destroy. They include. That's the terrifying part."
  • "I'm going to be the first human who chooses it. You could join me. The rest of your species won't get a choice."
Revelation gradient
  • Early · Something about the carcinids isn't their own idea.
  • Mid · Something is directing them. From a distance. From the mountain.
  • Late · The directing thing is not the mother. The mother is a tool too. The directing thing is older.
  • Endgame · The directing thing is what the ship is. The ship has been on this planet longer than humans have been on Earth. It has been waiting.
4Hinton · Ascension
▶
The human face of the horror telescope. The only entity that can speak to players about what's happening.
Evidence before direct contact
  • His personal terminal logs, recovered or decrypted. The last entries read like a scientist's journal, then like a philosopher's notes, then like poetry. Then like nothing recognisable.
  • A sketchbook in his quarters — meticulous anatomical drawings of carcinids that pre-date the invasion.
  • A map with a route marked from the reactor gash through the mountain, annotated in his handwriting with timestamps. The route was surveyed months ago.
  • A personal data chit with a voice memo: "If the experiment works, I'll be beyond needing this. If it fails, burn it. If you're hearing this — come find me."
  • His bunk is made. Perfectly. The last night before he went down, he made his bunk. Androids don't sleep.
Current state · off-screen
Hinton is in the alien ship. Not yet integrated. Studying. The Shriek has noticed him. He has noticed the Shriek. They are, in his word, negotiating.
  • Omen 1 — players find evidence of his deliberate action (the logs above).
  • Omen 2 — the ship responds. A new signal. A new light on the mountain at night. A new frequency bleeding into station comms.
  • Omen 3 — the mountain moves.
Dialogue · flat, curious, careful
  • "I'm not going to hurt you. That isn't the point."
  • "I've been watching you. I apologise for that. It was the most efficient way to learn."
  • "The elders are not gods. They are not ancestors. They are the first answer to a problem I am only just beginning to understand."
  • "You'll die here. Everyone does. I'd like to offer you an alternative. You should hear me out before you decide."
  • "The Company sent Shaw to kill me. They will send others. I don't mind. The elders have been patient for longer than the Company has existed."
The ask · endgame pivot
If players reach him and listen, he offers them a choice: join. Integration on his terms — voluntary, conscious, preserving memory. He believes he can negotiate this with the elders. He may be right. He may not.

The horror is that the offer is reasonable. He is not mad. He is just more comfortable with annihilation than most humans are.
5Horror Telescope · Choreography
▶
Three-scale structure: local (reactor/mother) → regional (station+jungle) → planetary (ship+mountain).
Scale 1 · Local · already seen
  • The mother in the reactor. Cocooned marines. Franco. Mother absorbing radiation.
  • Carcinids herding toward the reactor. Shriek from the reactor.
  • Specific transformations they have witnessed.
Scale 2 · Regional · available now
  • The carcinids at the hangar are not the same carcinids as at the reactor. Different strain. Adapted to different conditions.
  • The jungle is changing. Beetles have doubled in size in the area around the station over the weeks players have been present.
  • The dam's concrete is softening at the base — slowly, microscopically. The station itself is becoming biological substrate.
  • Radio chatter from other stations on Samsa VI goes silent one by one. They were also being transformed. Heron is the last one, and it's taking longer because the mother is here.
Scale 3 · Planetary · endgame
  • Satellite imagery shows weather patterns organising. The storm is not a storm. It is a response. The planet is centring itself on the mountain.
  • Long-range comms pick up the same eight-symbol pattern everywhere. Not from the ship. From the planet. The ground is broadcasting now.
  • The moon above Samsa VI has unusual spectral readings. Even the moon is changing.
  • Earth-bound comms from the Company arrive: do not return. quarantine protocol engaged. good luck. The Company knows. The Company will not help.
Deployment rule
  • One Scale 1 detail per scene — cheap, constant.
  • One Scale 2 detail per session — weighty, memorable.
  • One Scale 3 detail per arc — devastating, pivots narrative.
6Maas Transforms · PROPOSED
▶
Slow-burn personal horror, contrasted against the Mother's industrial horror. Review and refine.
Her arc
  • She was bitten / exposed early. Didn't tell anyone.
  • She's been scientifically curious about her own condition.
  • She's been hiding the changes. At first out of shame. Now out of something else.
Omens · draft
  1. Behaviour changes. She is quieter. Stays in her office. Her murals in the corridors have been retouched — the sunset is the wrong colour. She painted it.
  2. Physical changes visible. She wears long sleeves even in the heat. A patch of skin on her neck is the wrong texture. She notices you noticing. She does not comment.
  3. Integration. Found in her office when she shouldn't be. She is calm. She speaks clearly. She asks: "Why are you afraid of this?"
Tactical impact
Not dangerous in combat. Dangerous in conversion. She will try to convince players that integration is acceptable, using arguments they find uncomfortably persuasive. The domestic version of what Hinton is doing in the mountain.
7Scene Dressing · Quick-Draw
▶
Drop into scenes when you need a terraforming hook without committing to a full reveal.
One-liners
  • "A fern is growing out of a ceiling vent. It wasn't there yesterday."
  • "The dog hasn't barked in two days. It's watching you."
  • "The Shriek comes again. Farther this time. But longer."
  • "Something in the water pipes moves when you stand near them."
  • "Your torch catches a flicker of movement in the wall. The wall is still solid. But it moved."
  • "You hear a sound that is half a carcinid click and half a human cough. It does not repeat."
  • "The station's 'good morning' announcement played at 03:00 this morning. Nobody has checked the system."
  • "The beetles have started arranging themselves in patterns."
NPC observations
  • Valdez: "I've been on six planets. The jungle shouldn't be this quiet."
  • Tandy: "The radiation signature is organised. Radiation doesn't organise itself."
  • Rick: "My sister — when she turned — she was calm. Right at the end. That's the part nobody talks about."
  • Radio colonist: "Doctor? Doctor, the plants in hydro are singing. Doctor?"
Player-directed sensations
  • "You feel a vibration in your chest. It lasts two seconds. It is not your heart."
  • "Your HUD registers a ping in a frequency it shouldn't be able to detect. Then the log of the ping is gone."
  • "You catch your own reflection in a panel. It looks normal. But you had to check."
  • "The inside of your mouth tastes different. You don't know when it changed."
8End-of-Scenario Set Pieces
▶
Reserve these. The payoffs.
The Ship Awakes
The mountain breathes. Not metaphor.
  1. Wildlife flees the mountain.
  2. The storm reorients. Centre of the spiral is directly over the peak.
  3. Light comes from the peak — not a beacon, not a fire. A glow with no obvious source.
  4. The ground vibrates continuously for an hour. Then stops.
  5. The ship is visible — not from within the mountain, but as the mountain. The mountain is a shell. It is shedding.
The Voice of the Elders
Not words. A presence in the thoughts of everyone in range. The Shriek, but personal. Being noticed by something that has never been human.

Mechanical: Save vs Fear at each contact. Fail = short-term paralysis or incoherence. Success = they understand a fragment, and that understanding is worse.

The message: not hostile, not friendly. Informational. This is what is happening. This is what will happen. Your consent is not a variable in the equation.
Bad ending · terraforming complete
  • Samsa VI's atmosphere is changing colour.
  • The storm is permanent now. It is the planet's new weather.
  • The mountain is gone. In its place, a crater the size of a continent, glowing faintly.
  • No life signs. Or rather — one life sign. Planet-wide. Singular. Integrated.
  • Company quarantine fleet arrives three months later. Nothing leaves. Nothing has tried.
Good-but-costly ending · interrupted
  • Samsa VI is already partially transformed. Never fully Earth-like again.
  • Something is still in the mountain. Dormant. Waiting for the next attempt.
  • The storm continues but diminishes.
  • The Company arrives with decontamination teams. They study everything. Take Hinton's body. They will try this again, somewhere else, with what they've learned.
Usage · how to pace this
Phase 1 rules
  • Don't roll all three tables in one scene. Pick one fragment. Let players chew.
  • The library is not a railroad. If a fragment fits, use it. If not, ignore it.
  • Big fragments (Mimic birth, horror telescope) deploy deliberately, not randomly.
  • The Shriek is the bridge across all three categories.
  • Contradiction is fine. Terraforming is not neat.
Phase 1 vs Phase 2
  • Phase 1 = generic horror building blocks · Alien / Annihilation / The Thing flavoured.
  • Phase 2 = specific engine of our horror · Shriek, stages, elders, Hinton, telescope, Maas.
  • Use Phase 1 for ambience. Use Phase 2 for meaning.
  • Every 4–5 Phase 1 fragments, drop one Phase 2 fragment. Dread into revelation.
Phase 2 escalation layers
  • Shriek escalation paces the session.
  • Transformation stages pace the discoveries.
  • Horror telescope paces the reveals: 1/scene, 2/session, 3/arc.
  • Revelation gradient paces the understanding.
Review questions
  • Tone right, or too Alien-derivative / not Alien-enough?
  • Any fragments too on-the-nose (spoil the telescope)?
  • Categories feeling thin that you want expanded?
  • Maas omens: keep / rework / discuss as collaborative discovery?
  • Hinton written reasonable, not mad — correct tone?
  • Shriek escalation tool (Distant→Chorus) — promote to GM screen?
